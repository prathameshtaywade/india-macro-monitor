Inflation: Consumer Price Index

All-India, combined rural and urban, year on year. The band shows RBI's 2–6% tolerance range around the 4% target. The 2012-base CPI ends in December 2025; the 2024-base CPI publishes inflation from January 2026.

Source: MoSPI CPI (getCPIIndex, base 2012; getCPIData, base 2024).

Forex reserves and the rupee

Reserves are the total of foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs and the IMF reserve position. Exchange rates are RBI/FBIL reference rates in rupees per unit of foreign currency, so a rise means a weaker rupee.

Sources, refresh methods and known gaps

Where the data comes from

  • MoSPI eSankhyiki API (api.mospi.gov.in): National Accounts (GDP, GVA by sector), CPI on both the 2012 and 2024 bases, IIP on both the 2011-12 and 2022-23 bases, and two RBI tables it republishes: monthly forex reserves (from 1990) and monthly exchange rates (from 1992). Free, no key needed for reading.
  • RBI Handbook of Statistics on Indian Economy 2025-26 (released 31 July 2026): Table 40 policy rates and reserve ratios since 2008, Table 167 bank aggregates, Table 178 month-end G-Sec yields, Table 205 exchange rates, Tables 210 and 215 forex reserves.
  • RBI Weekly Statistical Supplement, weekly issues since January 2026: Table 2 forex reserves, Table 4 bank business, Table 5 rates (repo, call rate, T-bill, 10-year FBIL yield, FBIL USD/INR).
  • RBI press release of 7 October 2026 (MPC resolution, October 5–7): repo raised 25 bp to 5.50%, SDF 5.25%, MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%.
  • rbi.org.in "Current Rates" box for the snapshot table; each row shows its own date.

How to refresh

  1. MoSPI series: run python3 pipeline/fetch_mospi.py. Standard library only. The NAS endpoint takes one indicator per call; the CPI 2024 endpoint allows at most 100 rows per page, the others 200.
  2. RBI snapshot: run python3 pipeline/fetch_rbi_home.py.
  3. RBI Handbook and WSS tables: run python3 pipeline/list_wss.py to list the latest WSS file ids, paste them into pipeline/rbi_extract.js, run that script in a browser console on rbidocs.rbi.org.in, and save its output as data/raw/rbi_extract.json. rbidocs serves its XLSX files only to a JavaScript-capable browser. Add any new MPC decision to the script's policy list.
  4. Rebuild with python3 pipeline/build_data.py, python3 pipeline/export_dashboard.py and python3 pipeline/validate.py. On the website a scheduled GitHub Action does steps 1, 2 and 4 every day and publishes only if validation passes.
  5. Typical release rhythm: CPI around the 12th of each month, IIP near month-end, quarterly GDP at the end of the second month after the quarter, WSS every Friday, MPC decisions every two months, a new Handbook each year.

Known gaps and caveats

  • Base-year breaks. GDP growth on the 2022-23 base starts in Q1 2023-24, and it differs a lot from the old series where they overlap (Q1 2023-24: 6.6% new, 9.6% old). CPI inflation on the 2024 base starts in January 2026. Read the old and new series as separate lines, not one history.
  • Monthly RBI tables stop mid-2026. Handbook data end in March 2026 (bank aggregates) or June 2026 (yields, monthly exchange rates, weekly reserves). Later points come from the weekly supplement, which carries the 10-year yield, USD/INR and reserves but not the 1-year and 5-year month-end yields.
  • Two 10-year yields. The monthly Handbook figure is the yield on SGL trades at 10-year residual maturity; the weekly figure is FBIL's 10-year par yield. They track closely but are different measures.
  • Short bank history. Bank credit and deposits run from January 2022 in this build. Longer history is on RBI's DBIE portal.
  • Policy rate history starts in April 2008. Before May 2011 the repo rate was one end of the LAF corridor, not the single policy rate.
  • RBI DBIE (data.rbi.org.in) was not used directly: its API needs session tokens and encrypted requests and has no documented public access. Its downloads are the route to longer bank and yield histories.
